02 / KNOWLEDGE CORNER

Climate terms,
without the jargon.

A quick guide to the concepts behind low-carbon tourism. Start with the difference between asking travellers to contribute and compensating for emissions.

↗

THE CONTRIBUTION

Carbon surcharge

An additional charge linked to an activity or service, presented as a contribution toward climate-related action. Its purpose, use of funds, and accountability should be clearly explained.

A surcharge is a payment mechanism. It does not automatically prove that emissions have been reduced or compensated. Travellers need transparent information about where the money goes and how outcomes are verified.
◎

THE COMPENSATION

Carbon offset

Offsetting typically involves supporting projects intended to reduce, avoid, or remove greenhouse-gas emissions to compensate for emissions occurring elsewhere.

Offset quality depends on factors such as additionality, permanence, credible measurement, and avoiding double counting. Offsetting should not be presented as a substitute for reducing emissions where possible.
✳

THE BIG PICTURE

Net Zero

Net zero means balancing human-caused greenhouse-gas emissions with removals, after deep emissions reductions. In tourism, this requires action across transport, accommodation, food, and activities.

A credible net-zero pathway prioritises measuring emissions and reducing them over time. Any residual emissions and use of removals should be transparently accounted for.

Educational summaries only. Specific programmes and claims should be checked against their published methods and evidence.
